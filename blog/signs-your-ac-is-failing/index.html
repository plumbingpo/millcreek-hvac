Air Conditioner Warning Signs in Millcreek, UT

Home / Blog / Air Conditioner Warning Signs in Millcreek, UT

As the warmest months approach here in Millcreek, UT, our air conditioners become essential appliances of our home comfort. These units operate constantly to keep our indoor spaces cool and comfortable. But like any mechanical system, air conditioners will eventually wear down and will eventually begin to show signs of wear and tear, indicating that a significant issue or breakdown might be on the horizon. Recognizing these warning signs early can save you from a surprise system failure on the hottest day of the year, and potentially minimize damage in the long run. Here at Millcreek HVAC, we want to help you understand what to look out for.

Reduced Cooling Performance: Feeling Less Cool Than Before?

One of the most common and immediate signs that your air conditioner is struggling is a reduction in its cooling capability. You may constantly be tweaking the thermostat down only to still feel warm or humid indoors. Perhaps certain rooms are much warmer than others. This isn't just about comfort; it indicates a problem within the system. There are multiple potential causes for this problem. It could be a low refrigerant level, which means your system isn't able to absorb and release heat effectively. Refrigerant is the lifeblood of your AC. Another common culprit is a clogged air filter, which restricts airflow and makes the unit work harder without achieving the desired cooling. Dirty evaporator coils can also be a factor, as they cannot efficiently transfer heat. A struggling compressor, the core of the cooling cycle, might be the problem. If you're experiencing inadequate cooling, it's time to have a professional from Millcreek HVAC diagnose the problem.

Strange Noises: What Are Those Unusual Sounds?

Your air conditioner should operate with a relatively consistent, low hum. If you start hearing unusual or loud noises coming from your unit, it's a definite sign that something is amiss. A grinding or high-pitched squeal frequently suggests problems with worn motor bearings. These bearings, critical for smooth operation, can wear down over time, leading to friction and eventual motor failure if not addressed. Electrical issues, like a failing relay, can manifest as constant clicking. Loud banging or clunking is especially worrisome, as they might suggest a major mechanical failure within the compressor or fan. Ignoring these noises can lead to more extensive damage. Timely attention to these sounds can avert a minor problem from escalating into an expensive fix.

Increased Energy Bills: Why Is My Electric Bill So High?

One of the most insidious signs of a failing air conditioner is a creeping increase in your energy bills. When your energy consumption hasn't changed but your bills are spiking, your AC unit might be working harder than it should to achieve the same level of cooling. Various problems can contribute to this energy drain. As mentioned, a blocked filter reduces airflow, forcing the fan motor to work overtime. Leaky ducts can allow cooled air to escape into unconditioned spaces like your attic or crawl space, meaning your AC has to run longer to maintain the desired temperature. An aging or improperly sized unit may simply be past its prime. Low refrigerant levels increase the workload on your AC. Over time, components like the compressor and fan motors can lose their efficiency. If your utility costs are climbing for no apparent reason, it's worth having Millcreek HVAC inspect your AC unit to identify the source of the inefficiency. Our team can help you determine the most cost-effective solution.

Poor Airflow: A Trickle of Air, Not a Breeze?

Good airflow is crucial for effective cooling and even temperature distribution throughout your home. If you notice that the air coming out of your vents is weak, or if some rooms have a mere whisper of air than others, it's a clear warning of an underlying problem. This problem can stem from various causes. The most frequent cause is a heavily soiled or obstructed air filter, which restricts the volume of air that can pass through your system. Blocked return air vents can also hinder airflow. Problems with the fan motor, separate from the filter, can also cause weak air delivery. If the fan motor is weakening, it won't be able to push air through the ductwork effectively. Obstructions in the ductwork, such as debris or even collapsed sections, can also be a factor. Sometimes, even closed or partially closed dampers in your duct system can cause uneven airflow. Addressing poor airflow is important not just for comfort, but also because it forces your AC to work harder, potentially shortening its lifespan.

Excessive Moisture or Leaks: Puddles Around Your AC Unit?

While a small amount of condensation is normal during AC operation, excessive moisture or actual puddles around your indoor unit are not. This is a clear red flag that something is wrong. Usually, water leaks are due to an obstructed condensate line. The AC dehumidifies, sending collected water through a condensate line. Algae, dirt, or debris can block this line, causing water to overflow the pan. This can lead to water damage to your home, and in some cases, even electrical hazards. Leaks can also originate from a cracked or rusted drain pan. If there's a major refrigerant leak, ice can build up on the coils, melt, and cause overflow. If you notice any unusual water accumulation around your AC unit, it's important to call for service right away to prevent further damage. Millcreek HVAC can diagnose the source of the leak and provide a lasting solution.

Frequent Cycling: Is Your AC Turning On and Off Too Often?

Your air conditioner should typically run in relatively consistent cycles, turning on to cool your home and then shutting off once the desired temperature is reached. Rapid on-off cycles, or "short cycling," are a strong signal that your AC is in distress. In short cycling, the AC doesn't complete its cooling task, causing inefficiency and stress. This can be caused by several issues. An improperly sized unit (too large for your home) can sometimes short cycle because it cools the space too quickly and then shuts off before it can properly dehumidify. Often, short cycling is due to low refrigerant, a dirty filter, or a faulty thermostat. A failing compressor or an electrical issue can also lead to short cycling. Short cycling, regardless of its origin, quickly degrades AC parts, leading to early failure and higher bills. If your AC is short cycling, it's not operating efficiently or effectively, and it needs professional attention.

Old Age: How Old Is Your AC Unit?

Even without obvious problems, your AC's age is a key indicator of its approaching end-of-life. Most central air conditioning systems are designed to last between 10 to 15 years, with some well-maintained units stretching to 20 years. An AC unit nearing or past its expected lifespan will be more prone to issues and less efficient. Older units may rely on R-22 refrigerant, which is phased out and costly to maintain due to scarcity. The components within older systems are also simply more worn out from years of operation. While maintenance prolongs life, there comes a time when repairs outweigh the cost of a new, efficient system. If your AC unit is over 10 years old and you're noticing any of the other signs mentioned above, it's a good idea to start planning for a replacement. A new, energy-efficient model can offer significant savings on your utility bills and provide superior comfort for years to come.
