A little caution while you wait for us can head off a much larger repair. A frozen air conditioner should be shut off at the thermostat with the fan set to "on", which lets the coil thaw faster. Running it frozen can flood the drain pan and damage the compressor. A furnace that keeps short-cycling, or any burning smell, means it's time to shut the unit off at its service switch and call us right away.

How Logan Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep vegetation trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and schedule a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still inexpensive.

Take a look at the age of your system. That number tells you a lot. Most well-maintained air conditioners reach 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is past that range and also shows higher bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or frequent repairs, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install when the first heat wave hits.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Consider a weak capacitor: swapping it out is a short, inexpensive repair, but left alone for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a miserable night.
